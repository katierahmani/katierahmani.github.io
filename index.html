06 · Organizational diagnosis

Organizational Diagnosis - Bowling Alley

Case study

Interviewed leadership and employees to identify operational challenges in scheduling events, customer communication and internal knowledge gaps. Developed a low-cost, automated request form to standardize the event scheduling workflow. Streamlined staff and customer communication through automated email confirmations, request tracking and FAQ content.

Challenge

Event scheduling miscommunication: key details were missed, requests were lost, and some events were approved without review.

Approach

Interviews with the owner and employees to uncover pain points and the information the team needs to prepare for events.

Solution

A low-cost Google Form and Sheet workflow with automated confirmations, request tracking, and website FAQs.

01

Interview

Understand the problem

Interviewed the owner and employees to understand the alley’s main challenges. Miscommunication around event scheduling emerged as the biggest pain point, and it became the owner’s focus for the project.

What I learned
  • Specific details are needed up front. The team needs them to prepare the space and order food ahead of time.
  • The owner finalized every request because critical information was often missed. Employees left sticky notes asking the owner to contact customers and confirm dates.
  • The handoff broke down. The owner tried having employees gather general information, but they didn’t always collect everything, and sticky notes were sometimes misplaced.
  • Some events were approved without the owner reviewing them.
02

Create content

Standardize the request

Created an event request form so every request captures the information the team needs, collected in the same way every time.

Event request form fields
  1. Name (first and last)
  2. Email address
  3. Phone number
  4. Preferred event date
  5. Preferred time slot (e.g., 12–2 PM, 3–5 PM)
  6. Type of event (birthday, corporate, school outing, other)
  7. Estimated number of guests
  8. Age range of guests (if applicable)
  9. Preferred party package (if any)
  10. Special requests or questions
03

Recommend

Compile options for the owner

Compiled recommendations for the owner, starting with two options for the scheduling and event request workflow.

Two workflow options
Option 1Recommended
Google Form + Google Sheet

A simple, low-cost system. The form is reached through a QR code on the website, flyers, or at the front desk.

Benefits
  • No upfront cost
  • Easy to implement and maintain
  • Keeps control in-house
  • Scalable as volume grows
  • Can be enhanced later with automation (e.g., Google Apps Script)
Option 2
Commercial scheduling platform

A paid event scheduling or party-booking tool (e.g., Calendly, Acuity, Jane).

Pros
  • Professional interface
  • Some automation and calendar integration
  • May offer payment processing
Cons
  • Monthly fees
  • May not fit the alley’s specific needs (e.g., lane availability, party packages)
  • Customization may require technical support or added cost
How Option 1 works
  1. STEP 1Customer scans the QR code and fills out the request form (name, date/time, party size, contact info, etc.).
  2. STEP 2Responses populate a dedicated tab in a Google Sheet.
  3. STEP 3An automated email goes to:
    • The shared mailbox or all three points of contact, for review
    • The customer, confirming receipt and that someone will follow up within 24 hours
  4. STEP 4A staff member contacts the customer to finalize details.
Additional recommendations
  • Update the website with event scheduling information and FAQs.
  • Send an automated email after each request, letting the customer know a team member will contact them to confirm availability.
  • How to book an event
  • Cancellation policy
  • Food & drink policies
  • What’s included in event packages
  • Age limits & supervision rules
  • Event package pricing
Recommendation / Start with Option 1. It carries low risk and low cost, and it gives the business full control of its event scheduling process.
07 · Team climate survey

Volleyball Team Climate Survey

Case study

Developed and analyzed post-season surveys to gather player and parent feedback on the program climate, coaching feedback and player development. Recommended head coach align with athletic director to implement mentorship program for coaches, standardize practice schedules and streamline communication for parents and players.

Focus

Program climate, coaching feedback, and player development across a volleyball season.

Approach

Interviews, then two post-season surveys: one for players and one for parents and guardians.

Recommendations

Coach mentorship, standardized practice schedules, and streamlined communication for parents and players.

01

Interview

Understand the program

Conducted interviews to understand the volleyball program and identify what feedback would be most useful to gather. The conversations shaped the focus of the surveys: program climate, coaching feedback, and player development.

02

Create the surveys

Two perspectives

Built two post-season surveys, one for players and one for parents and guardians. Each opens with an introduction and survey tips encouraging honest, respectful, big-picture feedback, and each assures respondents the survey is anonymous. The surveys use a mix of question formats: multiple choice, agreement-scale grids, and open-ended questions.

Player surveyIntroduction, participation consent, and multiple-choice questions
Parent / guardian surveyThe same structure, with questions worded from a parent’s point of view
03

Review the results

Organize and analyze

Survey responses collected into a spreadsheet, with each response as a row and each question as a column. This kept the data organized and ready to review before the analysis began.

04

Deliver to the head coach

Recommend next steps

Delivered the findings to the head coach with three recommendations, including aligning with the athletic director to put them in place.

Recommendations
  1. RECOMMENDATION 1Implement a mentorship program for coaches.
  2. RECOMMENDATION 2Standardize practice schedules.
  3. RECOMMENDATION 3Streamline communication for parents and players.
Outcome / Recommended that the head coach align with the athletic director to implement a coach mentorship program, standardize practice schedules, and streamline communication for parents and players.
08 · Job redesign

Job Redesign - Organization and Relocation Service

Case study

Evaluated organizational workflows and recommended restructuring roles and responsibilities to address workload imbalance, improve scheduling coordination and increase employee retention through role clarity.

Challenge

A small home organization and repair company had concerns with retention, with one person managing two departments.

Approach

Reviewed how work was divided across the company, noting what works well and what may need changing.

Recommendation

Split the Operations Manager role into two positions so each department has focused leadership.

01

Understand the situation

Context and concern

The company is a small home organization and repair business concerned about retention. Its two functional areas, home repair and organization/clean-out, were both managed by one person.

02

Evaluate the structure

What works, what doesn’t

What works well

The company is divided into two functional departments, Operations and Handyman. This division is well done: the relationships between workers and the way tasks are allocated tie workers and workload together.

What may need changing

Given the demands on the Operations Manager role across two departments, it may be more effective to split it into two separate positions that align with the organization’s structure.

Current structure
One role, two departments

A single Operations Manager role covers both departments.

  • Operations
  • Handyman
Proposed structureRecommended
Two roles, one per department

The role is split into two positions that match the two departments.

  • Operations Manager
  • Handyman Manager
03

Recommend

Advocate for the division of roles

Provided recommendations for how a team member could advocate for dividing the roles.

Benefits of the change
  • Better alignment. Responsibilities and tasks shift from multiple people to better fit the organization’s needs and mission.
  • A manageable workload. Each area gets the attention and support it needs.
  • Retention and continuity. The role becomes more sustainable, supporting retention and continuity.
Outcome / Recommended splitting the Operations Manager role into two positions, Operations Manager and Handyman Manager, to create manageable workloads and support retention in the role.
